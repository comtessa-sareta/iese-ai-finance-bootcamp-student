OPERATOR: Our first question comes from Priya Anand with Kestrel Capital.

PRIYA ANAND (KESTREL CAPITAL): Congratulations on the quarter. Adrian, on the
supply agreement — a doubling of wafer allocation is significant. How should
we think about the revenue capacity that unlocks in fiscal 2027, and does the
inventory build pull any of that forward?

ADRIAN VOSS: Thanks, Priya. The wafer agreement roughly matches the demand
signal we see from committed backlog, so I'd characterize it as catching up to
demand rather than building ahead of it. We're not going to guide fiscal 2027
today, but the capacity supports a revenue level meaningfully above our
current run rate. On inventory, the build is concentrated in long-lead
materials for Corvus-2 — substrates and advanced packaging — so it converts as
the ramp executes.

PRIYA ANAND: And a follow-up for Ingrid: the DSO increase — is that a
one-quarter effect or the new normal given the hyperscaler mix?

INGRID SOLHEIM: Some of it is timing — shipments were back-half loaded this
quarter — and some of it is structural, because hyperscaler contracts carry
longer payment terms. I'd model DSO in the mid-60s going forward rather than
a return to the 50s.

OPERATOR: Our next question comes from Daniel Okafor with Northgate Securities.

DANIEL OKAFOR (NORTHGATE SECURITIES): Thank you. Ingrid, I want to push on

recurring feature of this business model? And what does the margin bridge back
to 60 percent actually look like?

INGRID SOLHEIM: It's a fair challenge, Daniel. Each ramp is discrete — last
year it was Corvus-1, this year it's Corvus-2 — so within any product cycle

ramping a new product every year will carry some structural ramp cost. What I
can tell you is that Corvus-2 yields are improving ahead of the curve we
assumed. Beyond the next quarter, we don't guide gross margin beyond the next
quarter, so I won't put a date on 60 percent.

DANIEL OKAFOR: Understood. And Adrian, earlier you said margins are
structurally improving — how do we square that with the CFO declining to
guide a recovery?

ADRIAN VOSS: There's no daylight between us, Daniel. Structurally, mix is
moving toward data center and software-attached revenue, which carries higher
margin. The pace of that improvement in any given quarter depends on ramp
timing, which is Ingrid's point.

OPERATOR: Our next question comes from Sofia Lindqvist with Alba Research.

SOFIA LINDQVIST (ALBA RESEARCH): Good afternoon. On the export license review:

impact. If the review concludes unfavorably during the third quarter, is the
practical effect that you would cut guidance by up to that amount? And why

INGRID SOLHEIM: We exclude it because the range of outcomes is too wide to
model responsibly — from full licenses granted to a subset denied. To be
precise: the 14 percent is trailing exposure, and roughly half of the affected
demand could be redirected to non-restricted SKUs over two to three quarters.
So the worst quarter-one impact would be meaningfully below 14 percent, but it
would not be zero. We chose transparency about the exclusion over a false
sense of precision.

SOFIA LINDQVIST: That's helpful. And the backlog — how much of the 6.8 billion
is from the affected region?

INGRID SOLHEIM: Approximately 900 million dollars, and those orders carry
standard cancellation protections.
